docs: the journal submission flow, end to end

A publisher's template is the specification. Two kinds are common and they are read
differently: a styled template by its style names (Elsevier's Els family, ACM's
Title/Authors/Abstract, Pandoc's reference.docx names), and a fill-in instruction
sheet by its text (word limits, keyword minimums, expected sections).

- references/journal-submission.md documents the flow: parse the template, write the
  content, build, then render and look at it. It lists what the generator does on its
  own and what still needs a person (a table spanning both columns, journal-specific
  blocks such as ElsArticlehistory, per-journal flourishes).
- SKILL.md points at it when the task is a journal submission.
- tests/cli-journal-flow.test.mjs runs the whole chain through the CLI — parse an ACM
  template, write content, build — and checks the document comes out. The library
  tests check pieces; this checks the thing a person actually does.

Verified by hand against a real Elsevier template as well: parse produced a
.template.json with 17 bound roles, and build produced a document whose title, authors,
affiliation, abstract and keywords all take the publisher's own styles.
